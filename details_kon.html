Problem

Established partners had reviews and sold well. Newer partners ran classes just as good, with none: generic photos standing in, no feedback loop after the class, nothing to point to. Trust across the marketplace was uneven, and add-to-cart rates showed it.

02

Goal

Give every event honest social proof, from its first day.

03

How I got there

Desk research, an expert call and journey mapping shaped the MVP. Each release after that closed one more gap: media, on-site collection, brand-new events.

The small seller gap

konfetti lists thousands of workshops and classes from independent partners. New and smaller ones often ran classes just as good as the top-ranked listings, and sold far less.

  • no customer videos or relatable photos
  • Competitive offerings but low sales compared to top ranking
  • AI-generated, stock or irrelevant pirctures
  • No customer feedback and conversation after purchase

A trust gap, not an offer problem

Photos and replies build trust

Customer photos signal authenticity; partner replies to criticism raise trust.

People read few reviews, and the bad ones

Most read about ten reviews or fewer and look for negative ones to judge honesty.

Expert call: German users share less

German users are less comfortable sharing photos in e-commerce profiles, linked to cultural differences in uncertainty avoidance (Knauß and Elsner, 2023).

May 2024

Photos, videos and partner replies

After the deploy in August 2022, we understood text alone couldn't fix events with weak visuals or vague descriptions, so the next release added a simpler upload split by product type, photo-first, plus a review gallery and public replies from partners.

Jun 2025

Reviews collected at the course location

The review email went only to the person who booked, so friends in the same group couldn't review, and it arrived days after the moment guests were most eager to share — and partners had no tool to ask in person. So partners now generate a QR code per course in the back office and print it as a flyer.

Guests scan it at the workshop, log in or sign up, pick the class they attended (filtered by partner) and leave a rating, text and photo.

2025–2026

One rating per event

  1. Level 1

    Event reviews

    Purchase and on-site reviews in one average. Only purchase reviews are marked verified.

  2. Level 2

    Partner rating

    From the partner's other events, labelled, with a tooltip saying the event is new.

  3. Level 3

    External rating

    Google rating with its source; it disappears as soon as konfetti reviews exist.

Rules and edge cases

One source at a time

Partner or Google ratings are never shown as event reviews, and never next to each other.

Verified means bought

On-site reviews count in the average but don't get the verified label.

Who can review on site

Every QR review needs a konfetti login, so it is tied to a real account, and each person can review an event only once.

Low ratings

Every review is shown, none hidden. Partners feared a bad review going live; instead of hiding it, the back office invites them to read it closely and reply in public.

Learnings

Culture shapes trust signals.

A photo that builds trust in one market can create distance in another. Checking with research before shipping saved a release.

Ask where the experience happens.

The best moment for a review was the end of the class, not the inbox days later.

Labels are part of the design.

A clear label on where a rating comes from builds as much trust as the rating itself.
